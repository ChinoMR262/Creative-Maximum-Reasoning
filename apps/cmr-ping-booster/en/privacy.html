02 Zero-Data Collection Principle

CMR Ping Booster does not collect or store personal data, browsing history, or DNS queries on CMR servers. To provide DNS resolution, required queries are sent directly to the public resolver selected by the user. That provider applies its own privacy policy:

  • Personal Identifiers: We do not request names, accounts, email addresses, contacts, precise GPS location, or hardware identifiers (IMEI/MAC addresses).
  • Browsing Data: CMR does not receive or store DNS queries, visited domains, network packets, or web sessions. Required queries are sent directly to the selected resolver.
  • Infrastructure: The application does not operate commercial proxy nodes, external routing gateways, or analytical tracking servers.

For Google Play taxonomy purposes, this ephemeral transfer is conservatively declared as Web browsing → Web browsing history. This does not mean that CMR creates or retains a history: CMR does not receive this data, and the query is sent to the selected public DNS provider to return the requested resolution.

03 VpnService declaration

The app declares the following use of android.permission.BIND_VPN_SERVICE:

SOLE TECHNICAL PURPOSE:

Android VpnService creates a local interface (TUN 10.254.1.2) that processes DNS queries. Allowed queries are forwarded over authenticated DNS-over-TLS to the provider shown in the app: Cloudflare, Google Public DNS, Quad9, AdGuard, or Control D. Results depend on the network, provider, and location; the app does not guarantee lower ping or fewer interruptions.

Clarification: CMR Ping Booster is not a remote VPN, does not mask the public IP address, and does not send general traffic to CMR servers. It does process DNS packets that Android directs to the local interface and forwards them encrypted to the selected resolver.
